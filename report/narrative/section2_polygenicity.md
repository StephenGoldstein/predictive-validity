### Do single-target drugs fail more often in polygenic diseases

For each non-oncology, non-infectious disease with a US-developed Phase 2 entry in 2008–2019, polygenicity was measured from its largest European GWAS with public summary statistics: the number of independent genome-wide significant loci in that study divided by the same study's SNP heritability on the liability scale (appendix). A disease whose genetic risk is spread over many small-effect variants scores high; one whose heritability sits in a few loci scores low. Of the {{polygenicity.cohort.diseases|int}} diseases, {{polygenicity.cohort.scored_diseases|int}} have a score ({{polygenicity.cohort.scored_entries|int}} of {{polygenicity.cohort.entries|int}} Phase 2 and 3 entries); the rest have no public summary statistics, no usable heritability estimate or no population prevalence, and are reported as a group of their own. The scored diseases were split into tertiles fixed before any outcome was joined (issue #147, comment {{polygenicity.comment_id}}), and three hypotheses were registered for the most polygenic tertile against the least: more stops because the target was not causal (P1), fewer Phase 2 advances (P2), and a smaller benefit of genetic support (P3). Each p is one-sided in the registered direction, and Holm's correction runs across the three.

**P1, not-causal stops: {{polygenicity.tests.P1.reading}}.** Phase 2 and 3 entries in the most polygenic tertile stopped because the target was not causal {{polygenicity.tests.P1.rr|x2}} times as often per entry as entries in the least polygenic tertile ({{polygenicity.tests.P1.ppv|pct0}} against {{polygenicity.tests.P1.rate_without|pct0}}; 95% range {{polygenicity.tests.P1.lo|x2}}–{{polygenicity.tests.P1.hi|x2}}; one-sided p {{polygenicity.tests.P1.p_one_sided|p}}, {{polygenicity.tests.P1.p_holm|p}} after Holm), on {{polygenicity.tests.P1.exposed_entries|int}} and {{polygenicity.tests.P1.reference_entries|int}} entries.

**P2, Phase 2 advance: {{polygenicity.tests.P2.reading}}.** Phase 2 entries in the most polygenic tertile advanced to Phase 3 or approval {{polygenicity.tests.P2.rr|x2}} times as often as entries in the least ({{polygenicity.tests.P2.ppv|pct0}} against {{polygenicity.tests.P2.rate_without|pct0}}; {{polygenicity.tests.P2.lo|x2}}–{{polygenicity.tests.P2.hi|x2}}; one-sided p {{polygenicity.tests.P2.p_one_sided|p}}, {{polygenicity.tests.P2.p_holm|p}} after Holm). The registered direction was fewer advances in the most polygenic tertile.

**P3, the genetics benefit by tertile: {{polygenicity.tests.P3.reading}}.** The interaction is fit only when each of its four cells holds at least 15 target sets and one event. Phase 2 entries with human genetic support for the disease (GWAS, gene burden or Mendelian evidence) number {{polygenicity.tests.P3.cells.top_genetics.entries|int}} in the most polygenic tertile ({{polygenicity.tests.P3.cells.top_genetics.targets|int}} target sets, {{polygenicity.tests.P3.cells.top_genetics.events|int}} advanced) and {{polygenicity.tests.P3.cells.bottom_genetics.entries|int}} in the least ({{polygenicity.tests.P3.cells.bottom_genetics.targets|int}} target sets, {{polygenicity.tests.P3.cells.bottom_genetics.events|int}} advanced), against {{polygenicity.tests.P3.cells.top_no_genetics.entries|int}} and {{polygenicity.tests.P3.cells.bottom_no_genetics.entries|int}} entries without support. The least polygenic cell with support falls short of the 15 target sets, so the interaction was not fit and Holm's correction keeps three tests. That cell is small by construction: the score's numerator is the locus count, so the least polygenic diseases carry the fewest loci and the fewest targets with genetic support. Within the most polygenic tertile, whose cells allow the fit, entries with support advanced {{polygenicity.tests.P3.genetics_within.top.rr|x2}} times as often as those without ({{polygenicity.tests.P3.genetics_within.top.lo|x2}}–{{polygenicity.tests.P3.genetics_within.top.hi|x2}}).

The {{polygenicity.descriptives.no_score.diseases|int}} diseases without a score ({{polygenicity.descriptives.no_score.entries|int}} entries) stopped because the target was not causal in {{polygenicity.tertiles.none.not_causal_rate|pct0}} of Phase 2 and 3 entries and advanced from Phase 2 in {{polygenicity.tertiles.none.s4_rate|pct0}}; most have no GWAS of the disease with public summary statistics, so they are the diseases genetics has least to say about (appendix). Polygenicity and disease area overlap: the tertiles split unevenly by area (appendix), and {{polygenicity.check.within_area_variance.share|pct0}} of the score's variance lies within area, so the area adjustment in each test absorbs part of whatever runs through area. Two sensitivities, adjusting for GWAS size and splitting all {{polygenicity.cohort.diseases|int}} diseases by whether a Mendelian form exists, are in the appendix with their readings: {{polygenicity.sensitivities.gwas_size.P1.reading}} (GWAS size) and {{polygenicity.sensitivities.mendelian_form.P1.reading}} (Mendelian form) for P1. A negative control, P1's model with the outcome a stop for a sponsor decision or an operational reason, read "{{polygenicity.sensitivities.negative_control.P1.reading}}"; an association there would point to confounding by area or sponsor interest rather than target biology. The score tracks GWAS size: its Spearman correlation with the chosen study's effective sample size is {{polygenicity.descriptives.gwas_size.spearman|x2}} over the {{polygenicity.descriptives.gwas_size.n|int}} scored diseases (appendix), so the null speaks to polygenicity as this score measures it, and the score is partly GWAS effort.
